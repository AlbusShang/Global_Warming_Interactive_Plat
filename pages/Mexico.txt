# Country: United Mexican States (Mexico, MEX)

## Region: North America  
## Status: Developing country  
## Current leader: Claudia Sheinbaum



## I. International Commitments

Mexico is a party to the **Paris Agreement** and has submitted updated nationally determined contributions (NDCs) under the UNFCCC framework. Under its current commitment, Mexico aims to:

- Reduce greenhouse gas emissions **by 35% below business-as-usual levels by 2030**;
- Increase emissions reductions further with international support and cooperation.

Mexico has also expressed long-term intentions to move toward net-zero emissions around the middle of the century.

Mexico participates actively in international climate negotiations and regional cooperation on renewable energy, biodiversity conservation, and climate adaptation.



## II. Domestic Policies

Mexico’s climate policy framework is based on the **General Law on Climate Change** and related national climate strategies.

The country has promoted renewable energy development, particularly solar and wind power, while also encouraging energy efficiency improvements and sustainable urban transportation systems.

Mexico has introduced policies to reduce methane emissions, improve industrial efficiency, and strengthen forest conservation and reforestation efforts.

However, fossil fuels remain highly important within the national economy, especially through the state-owned oil sector. Policy uncertainty in the energy sector has also affected renewable energy investment in recent years.



## III. Are These Policies Sufficient?

- Mexico possesses strong renewable energy potential, especially in solar and wind generation.

- Forest protection and methane reduction could provide substantial emissions reduction opportunities.

- Climate legislation exists at the national level and provides a general framework for mitigation and adaptation.

- Nevertheless, implementation has often been inconsistent, and emissions reductions remain insufficient relative to global climate goals.

- Continued dependence on oil and gas production weakens long-term decarbonization efforts.

- Stronger policy consistency, investment certainty, and emissions controls will be necessary to align Mexico with the goals of the Paris Agreement.
