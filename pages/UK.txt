# Country: United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland (United Kingdom, UK)

## Region: Europe  
## Status: Developed country  
## Current leader: Keir Starmer



## I. International Commitments

The United Kingdom is a party to the **Paris Agreement** and has established one of the world’s earliest legally binding net-zero frameworks. Under its updated nationally determined contribution (NDC), the UK aims to:

- Reduce greenhouse gas emissions **by at least 81% below 1990 levels by 2035**;
- Achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions **by 2050**.

The UK plays an active role in international climate diplomacy and previously hosted the **COP26 climate conference in Glasgow**. It also supports international climate finance, clean energy cooperation, and global carbon market development.



## II. Domestic Policies

Domestically, the UK’s climate policy is built around the **Climate Change Act**, which created a legally binding system of carbon budgets to guide long-term emissions reductions.

The UK has rapidly expanded renewable energy, especially offshore wind power, while almost completely phasing out coal-fired electricity generation. Carbon pricing is implemented through the **UK Emissions Trading Scheme (UK ETS)**.

The government also promotes electric vehicles, low-carbon heating systems, building energy efficiency improvements, hydrogen development, and carbon capture and storage technologies.

At the same time, challenges remain in decarbonizing transport, heating, aviation, and heavy industry sectors.



## III. Are These Policies Sufficient?

- The UK is widely considered one of the global leaders in climate governance and emissions legislation.

- Electricity-sector emissions have declined significantly due to coal phase-out and renewable energy expansion.

- Long-term legal targets and carbon budgets provide relatively strong policy certainty.

- However, emissions reductions in transport and buildings are progressing more slowly than required.

- Questions also remain regarding the pace of infrastructure expansion, energy affordability, and long-term industrial transition.

- Additional action will be necessary for the UK to remain aligned with the 1.5°C pathway and fully achieve its 2035 target.
