# Country: Japan (Japan, JPN)

## Region: East Asia  
## Status: Developed country  
## Current leader: Sanae Takaichi

---

## I. International Commitments

Japan is a Party to the Paris Agreement and has submitted multiple nationally determined contributions (NDCs). Under its current 2030 NDC, Japan aims to **reduce greenhouse gas emissions by 46% below 2013 levels by 2030**, with an aspirational goal of a **50% reduction**.

Japan has also announced a **net-zero greenhouse gas emissions target by 2050**. In addition, Japan has submitted new medium-term targets indicating reductions of **60% by 2035** and **73% by 2040** relative to 2013 levels, which the government presents as aligned with the global 1.5°C temperature goal.

---

## II. Domestic Policies

Japan’s domestic climate policy framework is guided by the **Plan for Global Warming Countermeasures**, the **Basic Energy Plan**, and most recently the **Green Transformation (GX) Basic Policy**. The GX Basic Policy is positioned as a central strategy to simultaneously achieve decarbonisation, economic growth, and energy security.

In practice, the GX Basic Policy places strong emphasis on technological pathways such as **nuclear power expansion**, **carbon capture and storage (CCS)**, and **ammonia and hydrogen co-firing in coal-fired power plants**. Japan has also announced plans to construct new-generation nuclear reactors, marking a significant policy shift.

At the same time, **renewable energy deployment has not been prioritised at the same scale**, and Japan continues to rely heavily on fossil fuels, including coal and liquefied natural gas (LNG). The government also supports overseas fossil fuel investments, arguing that gas can function as a transitional fuel.

Carbon pricing and emissions trading mechanisms have been proposed, but key design features remain unclear. The planned emissions trading scheme is expected to begin in 2026, while a carbon levy is scheduled for 2028, with concerns that both instruments may be too weak to drive significant emissions reductions.

---

## III. Are These Policies Sufficient?

- Japan’s greenhouse gas emissions have declined to their **lowest level since 2013**, largely due to reduced energy demand in industry and commercial sectors rather than structural decarbonisation.

- Under current policies, Japan is **projected to reduce emissions by only 31–37% below 2013 levels by 2030**, excluding LULUCF, meaning it is **likely to miss its own 2030 NDC target**, let alone a 1.5°C-consistent pathway.

- Japan’s **2030 emissions reduction target is rated “Insufficient”** by international assessments, as it falls well short of the **more than 60% reduction below 2013 levels** estimated to be compatible with limiting warming to 1.5°C.

- The **GX Basic Policy does not provide concrete emissions reduction trajectories** for either 2030 or 2050 and continues to promote so-called “clean coal” technologies, which are inconsistent with pathways required to limit global warming to 1.5°C.

- The continued reliance on **coal power with ammonia co-firing**, CCS, and fossil gas infrastructure risks **locking in carbon-intensive assets** and delaying the phase-out of unabated coal, which should occur by 2030 in developed countries.

- While Japan has announced ambitious long-term targets, **policy implementation remains misaligned** with these goals, and the current net-zero target lacks sufficient detail and credibility.

- As a result, international assessments rate **Japan’s overall climate targets, policies, and action as “Insufficient”**, with climate finance contributions rated **“Highly Insufficient”** and the net-zero target evaluated as **“Poor”**.
