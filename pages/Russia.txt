# Country: Russian Federation (Russia, RUS)

## Region: Eastern Europe / Northern Asia  
## Status: Developed country  
## Current leader: Vladimir Putin

---

## I. International Commitments

Russia is a Party to the Paris Agreement and has submitted a second nationally determined contribution (NDC). Under this updated NDC, Russia aims to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to **65%–67% below 1990 levels by 2035**, taking into account the **maximum possible absorption capacity of forests and other ecosystems**.

Russia has also adopted a long-term objective of achieving **carbon neutrality by 2060**.

---

## II. Domestic Policies

Russia’s domestic climate policy framework includes the **Low Greenhouse Gas Emissions Development Strategy**, which sets out the broad pathway toward carbon neutrality by 2060.

Government policy has emphasized measures such as **energy efficiency improvements**, the development of **electric transport**, and the use of **forest absorption** and other ecosystems as part of the national mitigation approach.

At the same time, Russia’s climate policy remains closely tied to its existing fossil-fuel-based economic structure, and the role of land sinks continues to be especially important in how the country presents its emissions pathway.

---

## III. Are These Policies Sufficient?

- Russia has adopted a formal long-term climate goal of **carbon neutrality by 2060** and has submitted a new 2035 NDC.

- However, the design of its target relies heavily on **forest and ecosystem absorption**, which reduces pressure for deeper emissions cuts in energy and industry.

- Independent assessments conclude that Russia’s target and policy framework do **not require sufficiently deep domestic reductions** in the near term.

- Climate Action Tracker rates Russia’s overall climate action as **“Critically Insufficient”**, indicating that its targets and policies are far from being aligned with the Paris Agreement’s 1.5°C limit.

- CAT also finds that Russia’s policy pathway remains weak even when compared with modelled domestic pathways, and that current action reflects only limited progress toward real decarbonisation.
