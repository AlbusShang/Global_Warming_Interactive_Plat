# Country: Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA)

## Region: Middle East  
## Status: Developing country  
## Current leader: Mohammed bin Salman



## I. International Commitments

Saudi Arabia is a party to the **Paris Agreement** and has submitted nationally determined contributions (NDCs) under the UNFCCC framework. Under its current commitment, Saudi Arabia aims to:

- Reduce emissions **by 278 million tonnes of CO₂ equivalent annually by 2030** compared with baseline projections;
- Achieve net-zero greenhouse gas emissions **by 2060**.

Saudi Arabia also promotes the concept of the **Circular Carbon Economy**, which focuses on reducing, reusing, recycling, and removing carbon emissions.



## II. Domestic Policies

Saudi Arabia’s climate policies are closely linked to the **Saudi Green Initiative** and broader economic diversification efforts under Vision 2030.

The country is investing heavily in renewable energy, particularly solar power, due to its favorable natural conditions. Saudi Arabia is also expanding research and investment in hydrogen production, carbon capture and storage technologies, and industrial energy efficiency.

Large-scale afforestation and land restoration projects have also been announced to combat desertification and environmental degradation.

However, Saudi Arabia remains one of the world’s largest oil exporters, and fossil fuel production continues to play a central role in the national economy and government revenue.



## III. Are These Policies Sufficient?

- Saudi Arabia has increased investment in renewable energy, hydrogen, and carbon capture technologies in recent years.

- The country has introduced clearer long-term climate targets compared with earlier policy approaches.

- Large-scale environmental restoration projects may contribute to adaptation and carbon sequestration efforts.

- Nevertheless, Saudi Arabia’s economy remains highly dependent on fossil fuel production and exports.

- Emissions targets are measured relative to projected baseline scenarios rather than fixed historical levels, making international comparisons more difficult.

- According to international assessments, Saudi Arabia’s current policy pathway remains insufficient to align with the 1.5°C temperature goal.
