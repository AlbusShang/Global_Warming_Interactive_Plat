# Country: Federative Republic of Brazil (Brazil, BRA)

## Region: South America  
## Status: Developing country  
## Current leader: Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva

---

## I. International Commitments

Brazil is a Party to the Paris Agreement and submitted a new nationally determined contribution (NDC) in 2024. Under this updated commitment, Brazil aims to reduce its **net greenhouse gas emissions by 59% to 67% below 2005 levels by 2035**.

Brazil has also stated a long-term goal of achieving **climate neutrality by 2050**. In international climate diplomacy, Brazil has increasingly emphasized climate justice, forest protection, and the role of developing countries in the global transition.

---

## II. Domestic Policies

Brazil’s current climate policy framework is increasingly linked to the national **Climate Plan**, which serves as the basis for implementing mitigation and adaptation targets. The government has also advanced the **Ecological Transformation Plan**, which aims to connect climate action with industrial policy, finance, innovation, and social development.

A central pillar of Brazil’s climate strategy remains the protection of forests and the reduction of deforestation, especially in the Amazon, because land-use change is one of the country’s most important emissions sources.

Brazil also has significant renewable energy potential and already relies on a relatively clean electricity mix compared with many major economies. At the same time, challenges remain in transport, agriculture, and in avoiding new fossil fuel expansion.

---

## III. Are These Policies Sufficient?

- Brazil’s updated **2035 NDC** is stronger than its previous commitments and is widely seen as a more ambitious international signal.

- Brazil has major structural advantages for decarbonisation, including high renewable energy potential and the possibility of achieving large emissions reductions through lower deforestation.

- The current government has placed greater political emphasis on climate governance, ecological transformation, and international cooperation.

- However, independent assessments indicate that Brazil is **still not on track** to fully meet the scale of emissions reductions required for a 1.5°C-compatible pathway.

- Climate Action Tracker currently rates Brazil’s overall climate action as **“Insufficient”**, noting that stronger implementation is still needed across sectors and that present policy trajectories remain too weak.

- A major long-term challenge is ensuring that progress in forest protection is matched by deeper action in **energy, transport, agriculture, and fossil fuel policy**.
